What Is the Quoridor Board Game?

Quoridor is an abstract strategy board game for two or four players, designed by Mirko Marchesi and published by Gigamic. Each player has a single pawn and a stock of walls. There are no dice, cards or hidden information, so every game is decided by planning rather than luck.

Its appeal is how little it asks of you. You can explain it in a minute, yet each turn poses a real question: do I step closer to my goal, or do I spend a wall to slow my opponent down?

Objective and Basic Gameplay

The objective: be the first player to move your pawn onto any square of the opposite edge of the board.

Players take turns, and on each turn you do one of two things:

  • Move your pawn one square up, down, left or right. If your opponent's pawn is directly in front of you, you can jump over it.
  • Place a wall in the grooves between squares to block movement. A wall can never be moved afterwards, and you may never seal off a player's last route to their goal.

That is the whole game. The step-by-step version is in How to play Quoridor, and every edge case is covered by the Quoridor rules.

What Comes in a Physical Game Set?

A standard Quoridor set is compact. The classic edition is made of wood and contains:

  • A 9×9 board of 81 squares, with narrow grooves running between them for the walls.
  • Four pawns, one per player in a four-player game. In a two-player game you use two of them, which start in the middle of opposite edges.
  • Twenty wooden walls, each long enough to span two squares. They are shared out equally: 10 each in a two-player game, 5 each with four players.

Many boards have storage for the unused walls, so the walls you have left are always easy to count.

How the Game Combines Movement, Blocking and Planning

Moving and blocking are two sides of one race. Your walk to the goal gets shorter as you move, while a well-placed wall makes your opponent's walk longer. The player who needs fewer turns to finish is ahead, and every turn is a choice about how to change that count.

  • Movement is steady but slow: one square per turn.
  • Blocking can add many steps for your opponent at once, but each wall is permanent and you only have so many.
  • Planning ties them together: when to hold your walls back, when to spend one, and what your opponent can do in reply.

Walls never capture or remove anything. They only reshape the board into corridors and detours, and because a wall may never completely seal a path, the best barrier is one that makes the opponent's shortest route much longer than yours.

Who Might Enjoy Quoridor?

  • Families and casual players. The rules are short and the pieces are easy to understand, so a new player can join in during the first game.
  • Chess and abstract-strategy fans. Perfect information and no luck mean that mistakes and good ideas both show up on the board.
  • People who like to out-think an opponent. Because walls turn the board into a shared puzzle, you are always weighing your own plan against what the other player might do.
  • Anyone short on time. A game is usually over in a quarter of an hour or so, and the set is small enough to carry.

If you prefer games driven by dice, cards or bluffing, Quoridor may feel too open. If you like a tidy puzzle with a human opponent, it is a good fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many people can play the Quoridor board game?

The physical game is for two or four players. In a four-player game each person gets 5 walls instead of 10. This website is for two players, which is the classic duel.

How long does a game of Quoridor take?

A typical two-player game lasts somewhere between 10 and 20 minutes, depending on how long each player thinks.

Is Quoridor hard to learn?

No. The rules fit in a couple of sentences: move one square or place a wall, and reach the opposite edge first. The depth comes later, from learning when a wall is worth more than a step.

Is there any luck in Quoridor?

None. There are no dice, cards or hidden pieces, and both players see the whole board at all times. The better plan wins.

Do the pieces have to be wooden?

No. Many sets, including the classic edition, use wooden walls and pawns, but the rules only care about the pieces' size and number, not the material.

Can I play Quoridor online instead of buying a set?

Yes. You can play online against another person, against the computer or with a friend on the same device in your browser, free and without an account. See the Play page for what is available. The rules are the standard two-player rules described in the Quoridor rules.
