How to Play Quoridor: A Beginner's Guide

What Is Quoridor?

Quoridor is a strategy game for two players on a 9×9 board. There are no dice, cards or hidden information: you win by thinking ahead.

The objective: be the first player to move your pawn to any square on the opposite edge of the board.

The twist is what you can do on your turn. You can walk toward your goal, or you can place a wall to make your opponent's walk longer. Choosing between the two, turn after turn, is the whole game. This page teaches the basics step by step; the Quoridor rules page is the full reference.

The Quoridor Board and Pieces

You need only three things.

  • The board. A grid of 9×9 squares (81 in total). Thin grooves run between the squares.
  • Pawns. Each player has one pawn. Here, Player 1 is the filled cyan circle and Player 2 is the white ring, so they differ by shape as well as color.
  • Walls. Flat pieces, each two squares long, that slot into the grooves and block movement across them.

How to Set Up a Game

  1. Start with an empty board.No walls are on it yet.
  2. Put each pawn on the middle square of its own edge.The pawns face each other: the fifth square of the bottom row for one player, the fifth square of the top row for the other.
  3. Give each player 10 walls.This is the two-player game. Keep them beside the board as your wall supply.
  4. Decide who goes first.On this site, the screen shows whose turn it is.

How to Move Your Pawn

On your turn you do one thing: move your pawn or place a wall. This section covers moving.

Step one square

  1. Pick a direction.Up, down, left or right. No diagonal moves apart from the special case below.
  2. Check for a wall.You can't move through a wall or off the board.
  3. Move one square.Then it is your opponent's turn.

Jump over the other pawn

If the opponent's pawn is on the square right next to yours, you can't share the square, so you jump straight over it and land on the square directly behind. That counts as your whole move.

Step diagonally when a jump is blocked

If a wall or the edge of the board sits right behind the other pawn, you can't jump. Instead you may move to a square beside the opponent's pawn, as long as no wall blocks that step.

How to Place Walls

Instead of moving, you can place one wall. A wall makes your opponent's route longer, which is just as good as getting you closer to the goal.

  1. Choose a groove.A wall is two squares long and lies in the grooves between squares, either across or up and down.
  2. Make sure it fits.It must sit entirely on the board and can't overlap or cross another wall.
  3. Keep every path open.After the wall is down, both pawns must still have at least one route to their goal edge. You may lengthen a route as much as you like, but never close it.
  4. Leave it there.Walls can't be moved or picked up again, and every wall you place leaves you with one fewer.

An illegal wall

The dashed wall below isn't allowed. The white pawn in the corner would have no way out, and a wall may never take away a player's last route.

How to Win Quoridor

The first player whose pawn reaches any square on the opposite edge wins immediately. You don't need to land on a particular square.

Beginner Tips

  1. Count both routes.Before each turn, work out how many steps you need and how many your opponent needs. Whoever is ahead should usually keep moving.
  2. Plan one step further.Ask what your opponent can do in reply before you commit to a move or a wall.
  3. Spend walls where they hurt most.A wall that adds one step costs you a turn and a wall. Look for walls that force several steps of detour.
  4. Balance moving and blocking.Only moving lets a good blocker catch you out, and only walling leaves you no closer to the goal.
  5. Save some walls.You have just 10, and a few in reserve let you answer a late run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who goes first in Quoridor?

Players agree or draw lots before the game. On this site, Player 1 (the filled cyan pawn that starts at the bottom) moves first in same-device and computer games, and the screen always shows whose turn it is.

Can I move or remove a wall after placing it?

No. Walls are permanent for the rest of the game, which is why it pays to think before you place one.

What happens when I run out of walls?

You can only move your pawn from then on. Your opponent can keep using any walls they have left, so don't spend all of yours too early.

Can a pawn jump over a wall?

No. Pawns only jump over the other pawn. A wall always stops movement across it.

Is Quoridor only for two players?

The board game also supports four players, with fewer walls each. The game on this site is the two-player version. The Quoridor board game overview explains the differences.

How long does a game take?

A typical game lasts around 10 to 20 minutes, and quicker if both players mostly race.

Can I practice before playing a person?

Yes. Play against the computer and pick Easy while you are learning the moves.
