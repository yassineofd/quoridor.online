How to play Quoridor

A friendly walkthrough for beginners: what you're trying to do, what you can do on your turn, and how a game is won.

The goal of the game

Quoridor is a game for two players. You win by being the first to get your pawn to any square on the opposite edge of the board. Your opponent is trying to do the same thing in the other direction.

The twist: on your turn you can either move your pawn or place a wall to make your opponent's journey longer. Deciding which to do each turn is the entire game.

Understanding the board

The board is a 9×9 grid of 81 squares. Between squares there are thin grooves where walls can go. Each player has one pawn and, in a two-player game, 10 walls.

Pawns start in the middle square of their own edge, facing each other across the board. Your goal row is the edge where your opponent starts.

Player turns

Players take turns, one action each. On your turn you must do exactly one of these:

  1. Move your pawn one square, or
  2. Place one wall on the board.

You can't pass. Players agree beforehand who moves first.

Pawn movement

A pawn moves one square at a time, up, down, left or right. No diagonal moves (apart from one special case below), and you can't move through a wall.

Jumping your opponent

If your opponent's pawn is right next to yours, you can jump straight over it to the square behind. If a wall (or the board edge) blocks that jump, you may instead step to a square beside the opponent's pawn, as long as no wall is in the way. The Quoridor rules spell this out precisely.

Using barriers

A wall is two squares long and sits in the groove between squares, blocking movement across it. Walls are permanent: once placed, they can't be moved or removed.

You can't place a wall on top of another wall, and you can never place a wall that completely cuts a player off from their goal. Walls make routes longer, not impossible.

Winning the game

The first player whose pawn reaches any square on the opposite edge wins immediately. There is no need to reach a particular square.

Beginner guidance

  • Count both routes. Before each move, ask: how many steps do I need, and how many does my opponent need?
  • Don't spend walls too early. You only have 10, and a wall that costs your opponent one step is rarely worth a turn.
  • Use walls that add several steps. The best walls force long detours.
  • Keep moving. A game of only walls leaves you with no progress at all.
