Quoridor rules

A structured reference to the rules of Quoridor, from board setup to the wall restrictions that keep every path open.

Board setup

The board is a 9×9 grid of 81 squares. Walls fit in the grooves between squares. In a two-player game each player has one pawn and 10 walls.

Starting positions

Each pawn starts on the middle square of its own edge. The two pawns face each other across the board. A player's goal is any square on the edge where the opponent started.

Turn order

Players alternate turns. On your turn you must do exactly one of the following:

  • move your pawn, or
  • place one of your walls.

Passing isn't allowed. Who goes first is agreed or decided by chance before the game begins. This site will always show who moves first.

Legal movement

  • A pawn moves one square up, down, left or right.
  • A pawn can't move diagonally, except for the special jump case below.
  • A pawn can't pass through a wall or leave the board.

Jumping

  • If your opponent's pawn is on an adjacent square, you may jump straight over it to the square directly behind, provided no wall lies between you and them or between them and that square.
  • If a wall or the board edge blocks that straight jump, you may move to a square beside the opponent's pawn (diagonal from your starting square), provided no wall blocks that move.

Wall placement restrictions

  • A wall covers the width of two squares.
  • It must lie entirely on the board, in the grooves between squares.
  • It can't overlap another wall, even partly.
  • It can't cross another wall at the middle (a horizontal wall can't cross a vertical wall at the same point).
  • Once placed, a wall stays for the rest of the game.
  • When you've placed all your walls you can only move.

Keeping paths open

You may never place a wall that leaves either player without any route to their goal edge. Before placing a wall, check both pawns: each must still be able to reach at least one goal square. For this check, only walls count as obstacles.

A wall that makes a route much longer is perfectly legal; a wall that closes it entirely is not.

Winning conditions

The first player to move their pawn onto any square of the opposite edge wins at once.
